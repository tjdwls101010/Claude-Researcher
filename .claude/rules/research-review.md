# Reviews and their dispositions

**Each finding has five slots because each slot fails differently.** Location (a claim id, a decision id, a run id, a `file:line`), observation, evidence, why it matters for acceptance, requested action. A finding without a location cannot be dispositioned; one without evidence is an opinion; one without a consequence cannot be ranked. When you read a review file, judge each finding on those slots before its severity.

**Proportionality cuts both ways.** Reflexive opposition is sycophancy with the sign flipped: a review that objects to everything gives 성진 nothing to prioritise and teaches them to discount the next one. `major` means "unfixed, a reason to reject"; a sound piece of work gets that said with the same precision as a defect.

**A field-norm critique must be citable.** "Reviewers expect five seeds" is a finding only with a paper, a saved source or a venue guideline behind it; otherwise it is a `note` marked as uncited, and a disposition may reject it on exactly that ground with the reference the critic lacked.

**Dispositions are the whole negotiation.** `accept` records what changed; `reject` needs a registry entry or a saved-source locator, because a rejection without evidence is the concession-scoring trick this harness declined; `test` names the discriminating run and closes only when it exists; `human` records that 성진 decided against the finding, with the Decision id. The gates read these, not your summary of them, so a major finding you addressed in prose but never logged is still open.

**Absence in the packet is not absence in the work.** The packet is assembled by the script and lists what did not exist ("no literature entries", "no runs"); a critic who reads an omission as a defect is reading correctly, and the fix is to add the thing, not to argue that it exists elsewhere.
