# Reviews and their dispositions

**Each finding has five slots because each slot fails differently.** Location, observation, evidence, why it matters for acceptance, requested action. A finding without a location cannot be dispositioned; one without evidence is an opinion; one without a consequence cannot be ranked. Judge a finding on those slots before its severity.

**Proportionality cuts both ways.** Reflexive opposition is sycophancy with the sign flipped: a review that objects to everything gives 성진 nothing to prioritise and teaches them to discount the next one. `major` means "unfixed, a reason to reject"; sound work gets that said with the same precision as a defect.

**A field-norm critique must be citable.** "Reviewers expect five seeds" is a finding only with a paper, a saved source or a venue guideline behind it; otherwise it is a note marked as uncited, and a disposition may reject it on exactly that ground with the reference the critic lacked.

**Dispositions are the whole negotiation.** A major finding closes only through a response the tool can check: the artifact that changed, the evidence that rebuts it, the sealed run that discriminated it, or 성진's recorded decision. The gates read the log, not your summary of it, so a finding handled in conversation and never logged is still open.

**Absence in the packet is evidence about the packet, not about the work.** The packet names what did not exist when it was built, and a critic who flags an omission is reading correctly. The response establishes why the thing is absent: if it exists elsewhere, bring it into the ledgers so the next packet carries it; if it does not exist, do the work. Arguing that it exists somewhere unrecorded closes nothing.
